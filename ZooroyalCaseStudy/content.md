# ZooRoyal — Improving Homepage Conversion

Founded in 2008, ZooRoyal has grown into one of Germany’s leading online pet retailers. With a catalog of approximately 12,000 products and more than 224,000 customer reviews on Trusted Shops, maintaining an average rating of 4.7/5 stars, the brand demonstrates strong customer loyalty and satisfaction.

For many pet owners in Germany, ZooRoyal is a household name—or, as my dog would probably put it, the place where treats come from.

In this case study, I propose a redesign of the ZooRoyal homepage with a clear objective: to strengthen conversion-focused Call-to-Action (CTA) elements, improve the visibility of promotional offers, and increase the number of users who progress to product detail pages and ultimately reach the checkout. By reducing distractions and surfacing high-intent content more effectively, the redesigned experience aims to better align the homepage with core eCommerce conversion goals.



---

## Finding 1 — The hero slot has no conversion purpose.

The entire above-fold hero is occupied by "ZooRoyal ist tierisch bunt" — a brand/pride banner with a rainbow stripe, no product, no CTA, no category entry point. This is the most valuable real estate on any start page. Every best-practice comparator uses it to deliver value: Zalando shows a live discount, AboutYou shows products, Decathlon shows sport categories. A new ZooRoyal visitor has no signal where to go or why to stay.

**Fix:** Replace the brand banner with a conversion-oriented hero — e.g. a featured offer with a direct CTA.

Measuring if it works by: scroll depth to first product section +25%

---

## Finding 2 — The login gate kills offer intent.

Every discount on the start page is walled behind account creation. The sequence: user sees "12% RABATT" → interest peaks → taps CTA → reads "Registrieren oder anmelden, um Coupon im Kundenkonto zu erhalten." → user leaves. The offer creates desire. The wall destroys it before any product is seen.

**Fix:** Show the discount code directly on the page. Move account creation to checkout — it's where high-intent users are most willing to register, not before they've seen a single product.

Measuring if it works by:  discount CTA click-through +35% · new user registration at checkout +18%

---

## Finding 3 — Category navigation is hidden in a hamburger.

New visitors have no signal that "Hunde", "Katzen", or "Kleintiere" exist as categories until they tap the hamburger menu. That's one extra cognitive step and one extra click before any orientation is possible. 

**Fix:** A persistent category tab row below the nav bar — visible on every scroll position, zero interaction required to discover it.

Measuring if it works by: homepage → category navigation clicks +40%


---

## Finding 5 — No product imagery — just brand and badges.

The ZooRoyal start page shows brand banners and illustrated discount badges with no actual products visible. The user has no visual anchor to connect their pet's need to a specific product or category.

**Fix:** Integrate real product photography into the start page — as a bestseller grid, recently viewed items, or category-specific product rows.

Measuring if it works by: time-on-page +15% · bounce rate −10%


## Priorization of Fixes: biggest reach, lowest effort first.

Now let's implement these improvements and add them to the shop roadmap. How should we prioritize them?
By reach and effort: the changes that impact the most users while requiring the least implementation effort come first. This ensures the highest business impact with the lowest cost.

1. Login gate -> Affects every visitor who sees an offer. 
2. Category nav -> Affects every session on every device and gives fundamental orientation.
3. Hero conversion -> important conversion trigger
4. Product imagery -> ? 


## Sum up & Next steps

Fix the start page and you increase the volume entering the funnel. The next audit layer is the category page and product detail page — the steps between start page and add-to-cart. After that: the checkout funnel.
