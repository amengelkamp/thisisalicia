Context

Why this read is different.

I'm a PO at flaschenpost — Germany's largest on-demand beverage delivery. My domain is invoice creation and payment processing: the layer directly behind a checkout button. When I look at a login gate on a discount offer, I'm not evaluating a UX preference — I'm evaluating a conversion decision at the layer I own.

This is a public audit benchmarked against four best-practice start pages: Zalando, AboutYou, IKEA, and Decathlon. No internal data. Five clear gaps.

Scope

Where the friction lives.

All five findings sit on the start page — before a user has entered the purchase funnel. Fix these and you increase the volume of users who reach product pages and checkout.

Start Page → Category → Product listing → Add to cart → Checkout → Payment

Finding 1

The hero slot has no conversion purpose.

The entire above-fold hero is occupied by "ZooRoyal ist tierisch bunt" — a brand/pride banner with a rainbow stripe, no product, no CTA, no category entry point. This is the most valuable real estate on any start page. Every best-practice comparator uses it to deliver value: Zalando shows a live discount, AboutYou shows products, Decathlon shows sport categories. A new ZooRoyal visitor has no signal where to go or why to stay.

Fix: Replace the brand banner with a conversion-oriented hero — category tiles for immediate navigation or a featured offer with a direct CTA.

Finding 2 — Biggest Gap

The login gate kills offer intent.

Every discount on the start page is walled behind account creation. The sequence: user sees "12% RABATT" → interest peaks → taps CTA → reads "Registrieren oder anmelden, um Coupon im Kundenkonto zu erhalten." → leaves. The offer creates desire. The wall destroys it before any product is seen.

Compare with Zalando: discount code GER15 is shown directly on the hero with a one-click copy button. No account required. No friction between interest and action.

Fix: Show the discount code directly on the page. Move account creation to checkout — it's where high-intent users are most willing to register, not before they've seen a single product.

Finding 3

Category navigation is hidden in a hamburger.

New visitors have no signal that "Hunde", "Katzen", or "Kleintiere" exist as categories until they tap the hamburger menu. That's one extra cognitive step and one extra click before any orientation is possible. Zalando shows "Bekleidung · Schuhe · Accessoires" as sticky inline tabs directly below the logo — zero clicks, instant orientation on every page.

Fix: A persistent category tab row below the nav bar — visible on every scroll position, zero interaction required to discover it.

Finding 4

The carousel buries conversion-critical offers.

Three active discount offers sit inside a carousel. Slides 2 and 3 are invisible to anyone who doesn't actively swipe — which is the majority of users. A carousel is a valid pattern for brand or editorial content. It's the wrong pattern for offers that compete for purchase intent.

Fix: Stack all active offers as visible cards below the hero. Every deal is seen on first scroll. No swipe required. The carousel can stay for brand content above — offers move below as a permanent strip.

Finding 5

No product imagery — just brand and badges.

The ZooRoyal start page shows brand banners and illustrated discount badges with no actual products visible. AboutYou, IKEA, and Decathlon all show real product photography above the fold. Without product signals, category intent cannot form — the user has no visual anchor to connect their pet's need to a specific product or category.

Fix: Integrate real product photography into the start page — as a bestseller grid, recently viewed items, or category-specific product rows. Products sell products.

Prioritisation

Fix order: biggest reach, lowest effort first.

Five findings, one backlog. Ordered by reach — the fix that affects the most users with the least implementation cost goes first.

Login gate → Category nav → Stack offers → Hero conversion → Product imagery

First — Finding 2

Remove the login gate

Affects every visitor who sees an offer. Show code directly, move account creation to checkout. Highest reach, lowest effort — likely a config change plus legal sign-off.

~1 sprint · Config + legal review

Second — Finding 3

Visible category navigation

Sticky category row below nav. One frontend component. Affects every session on every device — orientation is the first UX job of any start page.

~1 sprint · Frontend component

Third — Finding 4

Stack the offers

Replace carousel with stacked cards for the offer section. All deals visible on first scroll. Low engineering effort — slides 2 and 3 currently have near-zero reach.

~1–2 sprints · Layout change

Fourth — Findings 1 + 5

Hero + product imagery

Bigger redesign: replace the brand hero with conversion content and add product photography. Requires design, content pipeline, and cross-team alignment.

~3 sprints · Design + content

What's Next

The start page is the entry point. The funnel goes deeper.

Fix the start page and you increase the volume entering the funnel. The next audit layer is the category page and product detail page — the steps between start page and add-to-cart. After that: the checkout funnel.

→

Category page UX

Filter usability, sorting defaults, product card density — the decisions that determine whether browsing converts to intent.

→

Product detail page (PDP)

Add-to-cart affordance, subscription nudge, cross-sell placement. The PDP is where intent either converts or dies.

→

Checkout funnel

Cookie wall blocking the session, invisible add-to-cart button, 3-click cart-to-checkout tax — documented separately in the ZooRoyal Checkout Audit.
