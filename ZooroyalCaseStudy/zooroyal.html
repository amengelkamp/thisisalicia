Case Study · ZooRoyal E-Commerce

I own payment processing at an e-commerce company. When I walked ZooRoyal's checkout, I counted three moments where I would have lost a user.

A product owner's audit of the add-to-cart → payment funnel. No internal data. Three public signals. One prioritised roadmap.

Context

Why this read is different.

I'm a PO at flaschenpost — Germany's largest on-demand beverage delivery service. My domain is invoice creation and payment processing: the layer that sits directly behind a checkout button. That means when I audit a checkout funnel, I'm not just evaluating what a user sees — I'm evaluating what each friction point costs at the layer I own.

This is a public-facing audit. I triangulated findings from three sources:

01

Baymard Institute benchmarks

Industry research on checkout UX and cart abandonment — the credibility anchor when internal data isn't available.

02

Playwright browser walkthrough

Automated end-to-end journey through the live shop — homepage to checkout — with video capture at each step.

03

Direct user observation

Walking through the shop as a first-time customer and annotating friction points, cognitive load, and dead-ends.

Scope

The funnel I audited.

The shop feels fine — which is exactly why it's dangerous. Invisible friction doesn't register to a casual user. It only shows up in funnel drop-off data. All three findings sit in the highest-impact zone for conversion.

Homepage → Category → Product listing → Add to cart → Cart → Checkout → Payment

Finding A blocks before the funnel even starts. Findings B and C are in the highlighted steps.

Finding A

The blocked session.

Every new session — first-time visitors, incognito browsers, paid acquisition clicks — hits a hard wall before they can interact with the page. The Usercentrics cookie consent widget loads asynchronously via shadow DOM and intercepts all pointer events for 10–15 seconds. No click, scroll, or navigation is possible until it resolves.

Current

Improved

Non-blocking slim banner at the bottom of the screen

The page loads fully interactive. The consent UI renders as a slim bar at the bottom — visible but non-intrusive. Users can scroll, browse, and engage immediately. Consent is stored after first interaction; returning visitors are never interrupted again. GDPR-compliant: no data processed until consent is given.

Target metric: first-session bounce rate −10% · time-to-first-interaction −12s

Baymard Institute: consent banners that block page interaction increase bounce rates by 12–23% on first-session landing pages. Every euro spent on paid acquisition currently lands on a blocked experience.

Finding B

The invisible button.

The add-to-cart button on product listing pages is icon-only and hidden until hover. On desktop, it requires a hover interaction to appear. On mobile — roughly 60% of German e-commerce sessions — hover states don't exist. The button is effectively invisible to touch users.

Current

Improved

Persistent labelled CTA — visible on all devices without interaction

Replace the hover-only icon with a persistent "In den Warenkorb" button below the product price. Always visible, always clickable — same experience on touch as on desktop. The label makes the action unambiguous. No discovery required.

Target metric: add-to-cart click rate from product listing +15%

Baymard Institute: "unclear or missing add-to-cart affordance" is a top-10 mobile UX failure. On mobile (≈60% of German e-commerce sessions, Statista 2024), hover-dependent UI patterns create a complete dead zone.

Finding C

The 3-click tax.

Between adding a product to the cart and reaching the checkout form, a user must make three navigational decisions: click the cart icon, navigate through a side panel, reach the full cart page, then click "Zur Kasse". Each step introduces a moment to reconsider, a page load, and a chance to leave.

Current

Improved

"Direkt zur Kasse" CTA in the cart side panel — skip the intermediate page

Add a primary checkout CTA directly in the cart side panel. High-intent users skip the intermediate cart page entirely — one click from add-to-cart to checkout. The full cart page remains accessible via a secondary link for users who want to review or change their order.

Target metric: cart-to-checkout conversion rate +20%

Baymard Institute: the average optimised checkout has 14.88 form fields vs. an industry average of 23.48. Each unnecessary navigational step removes approximately 20% of remaining purchase intent. Reducing from 3 clicks to 2 is a revenue decision, not a UX preference.

Prioritisation

What I'd tackle first.

Three findings, one backlog. Ordered by impact on the broadest set of users, not by effort — because starting with what moves the needle fastest is how you earn the trust to do the harder work next.

First — Finding B

Persistent add-to-cart CTA

Affects every device, every session, every product listing. The highest-reach fix in the backlog — and the clearest success metric: add-to-cart click rate by device type.

~2 sprints · UI component + A/B test

Second — Finding A

Non-blocking consent banner

Lower effort than B or C — it's a Usercentrics configuration change plus legal review. Impact is concentrated on new users and paid acquisition: the sessions most likely to be cold and bounce-prone.

~1 sprint · Config + legal sign-off

Third — Finding C

Direct checkout from cart panel

Highest conversion impact but requires frontend rework across the cart flow — panel, cart page, checkout entry point. Do this after B and A have generated data and trust with stakeholders.

~3 sprints · Panel redesign + regression

Out of scope

Visual design refresh

The shop looks "a bit dated" — but subjective perception isn't a conversion metric. Defer until findings A–C have shipped and proven their impact.

Revisit in Q4 if KPIs warrant

Roadmap

90 days.

→

Days 1–30 · Persistent add-to-cart CTA (Finding B)

PO + Frontend + UX. Replace hover-only icon with persistent labelled button. A/B test: existing icon vs. labelled CTA, split by device type. Success: add-to-cart rate from PLP +15%, mobile parity with desktop.

→

Days 30–60 · Non-blocking consent banner (Finding A)

PO + Analytics + Legal. Reconfigure Usercentrics to render as a non-blocking bottom bar. Legal review for GDPR compliance. Success: first-session bounce rate −10%, time-to-first-interaction −12s.

→

Days 60–90 · Direct checkout from cart panel (Finding C)

PO + Full-stack + UX. Add primary "Direkt zur Kasse" CTA to cart side panel. Retain cart page as secondary option. Success: cart-to-checkout conversion rate +20%, validated via cohort split new vs. returning users.

Validation

How I'd validate this with your data.

This audit is a hypothesis, not a verdict. Every finding above is based on public signals — the moment I have access to ZooRoyal's actual funnel data, here's exactly where I'd look first.

A

Session start → first interaction time

How long does the average new session take to register its first click? Filter by device type and acquisition source. A spike on mobile or paid traffic confirms the cookie wall hypothesis.

B

PLP add-to-cart rate by device

Is the desktop add-to-cart rate significantly higher than mobile? A large gap is the fingerprint of a hover-dependent UI pattern. If mobile rate is within 20% of desktop, the finding is weaker than assumed.

C

Cart panel → checkout conversion

What percentage of users who open the cart side panel proceed to the checkout form? How many drop off at the intermediate cart page? A high drop rate at that step validates the 3-click hypothesis directly.

I'd love to see where the data agrees — and where it surprises me.
