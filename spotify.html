Problem

Spotify has all the data. It just doesn't connect the dots.

Spotify knows your listening history with extraordinary precision — every artist, every play, every hour. It also has concert data: a dedicated page per artist, populated with tour dates and ticket links.

But the two are never combined. There is no surface that says: "These artists you listen to constantly are playing near you next month." The data to make that personal is all there — it's a product prioritisation gap, not a technical one.

The result: users who would happily attend concerts miss them entirely. Not because they didn't care, but because manually checking artist pages for tour dates — for every artist you follow — is friction nobody bothers with.

Solution

Three design principles guided every decision.

01

Listening signal as the filter, not genre or location alone

Concerts are ranked by total listening time over the last 12 months — not all-time (one obsessive week skews it) and not raw play count (too noisy). 12 months balances recency with stability. The ranking is the personalisation; the radius just sets the geography.

02

A row, not a tab — placement earns its position

Concerts live as a horizontal scroll row on the home screen, below personalised content. Not a top-level tab — that pattern is reserved for content-type filters (Musik, Podcasts), not discovery destinations. The row only renders when there's at least one match. It disappears silently if there's nothing to show.

03

Earn the notification opt-in at the highest-intent moment

The notification prompt appears the first time the concerts row appears on screen — not on first app launch, not buried in settings. That's the moment the user has just experienced the value. It's the only moment where "yes, tell me about this" is an obvious answer.

The Mockup

Try it yourself.

An interactive prototype built in plain HTML and CSS — no framework. The prototype is modelled on Spotify's German locale (Spotify DE). Click through the full flow: watch the concerts row animate in, adjust the radius, explore the Europa-Show exception, and open a festival detail page.

Key Decisions

Five choices worth explaining.

1

12 months, not all-time listening

All-time totals are dominated by whatever you were obsessed with years ago. 12 months captures real current taste while smoothing out single-week spikes. It's the same window Spotify uses for Wrapped — which means the ranking is already familiar to users even if they can't name the logic.

2

The "Einzige Europa-Show" exception rule

If an artist is in the user's top 5 by listening time and has three or fewer European tour dates, show the concert regardless of radius. A user who listens to Phoebe Bridgers for 200 hours a year would probably travel to Berlin for her only European show — the radius shouldn't silently hide that. The label "Einzige Europa-Show" does the persuasion work; the system never expands radius without telling you.

3

One card per festival

A festival with four of your favourite artists showing up as four separate cards would create noise and bury single-artist shows. One festival card, ranked by combined listening time of all matching artists, with the top three named on the card. The full lineup lives on the detail page.

4

Sold-out shows stay visible

Hiding sold-out shows removes real signal — a sold-out show tells you the artist has demand near you, and resale tickets often exist. Sold-out entries get a greyed treatment and a badge. If total results exceed 10, a toggle appears to hide them. The user controls this, not the algorithm.

5

The detail page earns the redirect

Tapping "Tickets kaufen" sends the user to Ticketmaster. But the detail page comes first: date, venue, distance, support acts, map, price. The user should arrive at Ticketmaster already decided — not still figuring out whether they want to go. The redirect is the last step, not the first.

Technical Approach

Two APIs, one meaningful combination.

The listening signal comes from Spotify's Web API — specifically /me/top/artists with time_range=medium_term (approximately 6 months, the closest available window to the 12-month target). Concert data comes from the Ticketmaster Discovery API or Bandsintown — Spotify's concert data is not exposed in their public API.

The matching logic is straightforward: fetch the user's top 50 artists, call the concert API for each, filter by radius, sort by date with listening rank as a tiebreaker. The Europa-Show exception runs as a post-filter pass on the top-5 artists.

Note: Spotify has been progressively restricting public API access since 2024. Verify current endpoint availability before building — the endpoints referenced here were available at time of writing but the landscape is shifting.

What I'd Measure

How we know it's working.

1

Click-through rate on the concerts row

% of users who see the row and tap at least one card. Baseline against similar discovery rows (artist radio, playlist recommendations). If CTR is below baseline, the ranking signal isn't personal enough.

2

Notification opt-in rate

% of users who see the prompt and tap "Ja, benachrichtigen." Placed at the highest-intent moment — this number sets the ceiling for how many users benefit from the follow-on notification system. Target: above 40%.

3

Ticketmaster redirect rate

% of detail page views that result in a tap on "Tickets kaufen." This measures whether the detail page earns the redirect. Low rate = the page isn't convincing enough or the user decided against it after seeing price/distance.

4

Radius adjustment rate

% of users on the full concerts page who change the default 150 km radius. High rate = the default is wrong for a significant segment. Low rate = 150 km is working. Segment by city size — rural users likely need a wider default.

What's Next

The MVP leaves one obvious gap.

The post-show experience is completely absent. A user who tapped "Tickets kaufen," went to the concert, and came back to Spotify the next day sees nothing different. That's a missed moment.

→

Post-show setlist replay

If the user attended a show (inferred from location + ticket purchase signal), surface a playlist of what the artist actually played that night. The fandom spike is highest in the 24 hours after a show — that's the moment to deepen the relationship, not a week later.

→

Friend activity on concerts

Spotify already shows what friends are listening to. Extending that to "3 friends are going to this" adds social proof to the ticket decision — the highest-converting trigger for live event attendance.

→

In-app ticket purchase

The MVP redirects to Ticketmaster — necessary for launch speed, but it breaks the flow and loses attribution. A deeper partnership or white-label integration would keep the purchase in-app and give Spotify direct conversion data.
